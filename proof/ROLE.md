# Role — what I claim, and what I refuse to claim

Hiring teams get burned by candidates who inflate AI work. This page exists so nobody has to guess.

## I claim

- **Direction:** I chose the architectures, constraints and acceptance criteria for three custom
  non-attention ML systems.
- **Verification design:** I wrote the evidence standards, selftest-with-mutants methodology, and
  release gates those systems were judged against.
- **Operation:** I run a production estate of ~2.8M lines of Python and ~222k lines of Go on
  self-hosted infrastructure, and I hold the release sign-off.
- **Translation:** seven years teaching, including coaching adults through new systems; I can explain
  a complex system to the person who has to use it tomorrow.

## I do not claim

- That I wrote the implementation code. AI agents implemented under my direction.
- Proficiency in Python, Go, Lean 4, Coq or Isabelle. I read Python and Go; I write rudimentary HTML
  and some Python; I understand formal verification at the design level only.
- Benchmarks or trading performance beyond the recorded results cited in `EVIDENCE.md`.
- Customer logos, revenue figures, or funding I do not have documentation for.

## What an employer gets

Someone who can sit with a technical team and a non-technical customer, write the thing down so both
agree what "done" means, build the checks that catch AI work that is confidently wrong, and teach it
until it sticks. That is the job description for enablement, adoption, implementation and customer
education roles — and it is what the last year of my life has actually been.
